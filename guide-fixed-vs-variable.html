Fixed vs variable rate — which is better?

Every lender will offer you both. The honest answer is neither is "better" in general — they trade certainty for potential savings in opposite directions.

Fixed rate: you know exactly what you'll pay

Your interest rate is locked for the entire loan term. Your payment never changes because of the market — only because of things like escrow adjustments for taxes or insurance, if those are bundled in. This is the safer, more predictable choice, especially for anyone on a tight or fixed budget.

Variable rate: you're betting on the market

Also called adjustable-rate, this starts lower than a comparable fixed rate — sometimes noticeably lower — but it can rise (or fall) after an initial period, tied to a benchmark rate. If rates drop, you win. If they climb, so does your payment, sometimes by a lot.

How to actually decide

  • If you plan to stay in the loan long-term and value predictability, fixed is usually the safer default
  • If you plan to sell or refinance before the adjustable period kicks in, a variable rate's lower starting cost can genuinely save you money
  • If your budget has little room for a higher payment, don't gamble on variable — a rate hike could hurt more than the initial savings helped

Run both scenarios through the mortgage calculator using the highest rate your variable loan could reach, so you're planning for the worst case, not just the best one.
