How to pay off your loan faster

You don't need to refinance or find extra income to cut years off a loan. A few small, consistent habits do most of the work — because of how interest compounds on your remaining balance.

Make one extra payment a year

Putting just one additional full payment toward principal each year — spread monthly or as a lump sum — can shave several years off a 30-year mortgage. It works because that extra money reduces the balance interest is calculated on, for every month afterward.

Round up every payment

Rounding a $1,860 payment up to $2,000 doesn't feel like much, but applied consistently, that small overpayment compounds the same way — steadily lowering the balance faster than the schedule calls for.

Apply windfalls directly to principal

A tax refund, bonus, or gift used to pay down principal (not just sent as a regular payment) has an outsized effect early in the loan, when the most interest is being charged.

Confirm there's no prepayment penalty

Most mortgages don't have one, but some personal and auto loans do. Always check before making extra payments — a penalty could offset the interest you're trying to save.

Try adding an extra $100-200 a month into the mortgage calculator's term field to see roughly how many years it could cut off.
